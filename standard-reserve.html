01 · The big idea

A closed economy with four moving parts

Think of it as Olympus-style reserve currency with one fix: instead of printing on a fixed schedule, it prints more when capital flows in and clamps down when capital flows out.

Currency

$STANDARD

ERC-20, 1B hard cap. Minted on demand, burned constantly.

Market

One Pool

ETH ⇄ STANDARD on Uniswap v4 with a custom hook. Every trade is measured here.

Signal

Net ETH Flow

ETH in from buys minus ETH out from sells. Real capital, not trade counts.

Authority

The Bank

~4,000 lines of non-upgradeable code. It sets issuance and routes fees.

Expansion: capital flows in → issuance rises, fees buy hard reserves (tokenized gold). Contraction: capital flows out → issuance is cut, fees buy back and burn STANDARD, and exits get expensive.

03 · The currency

1 billion cap. Almost nothing pre-minted.

Issuance is only a ledger entry until a banker withdraws. Tokens are actually minted at that single moment, and a lot of the system's actions remove supply for good.

Removes supply

Ledger removals

Expansion licenses (100% burned), plus the burned half of resolution and revocation fees. Never minted.

Token burns

Open-market buybacks by the contraction vault, plus token-side fees from the protocol's own liquidity.

When it ends

Budget exhausted

Once cumulative issuance hits 900M, base issuance stops for good and the economy runs on recycled fees.

The mintable ceiling can only go down: every burn and ledger removal lowers the max supply that can ever exist.

04 · Monetary policy

Cuts are fast. Raises are earned.

Every 3-day epoch the bank checks net ETH flow and adjusts the issuance multiplier m between 0.2× and 1.25×. Build your own market history below.

Rule: negative net flow → m drops 0.15 immediately (floor 0.2). Positive flow for 2+ consecutive epochs → m rises 0.10 each epoch (ceiling 1.25). The real signal sums the last two epochs' flow; this tool simplifies to one sign per epoch to show the shape. Ceiling in 12 days of sustained inflow, floor in about 3 weeks of sustained exodus.

05 · Bankers

A charter is a company. Branches are its stores.

Charter

Soulbound NFT that makes you a banker. 1,000 Founding Charters at genesis. Later ones sell by daily ETH Dutch auction.

Branches

Start with 1, max 10. Each branch earns an equal slice of daily issuance. More branches, bigger cut.

Licenses

Extra branches cost an expansion license, paid in STANDARD and burned on the spot.

06 · Auctions

Price starts high and falls until someone bites

Licenses sell in 12-hour Dutch auctions (50 per period, max 3 per charter per day). The opening price is 2× the last close and the gap to the floor halves every 2 hours.

Wait for a cheaper price and risk the 50 licenses selling out first. Each period's final sale anchors the next opening, so the market keeps re-pricing from demand. Charter auctions work the same way in ETH: they open at 3× the last sale with a 4-hour half-life.

07 · Exiting

Leaving costs more when everyone leaves

To cash out you retire a branch, which permanently cuts your future yield. The resolution fee rises with system-wide exit pressure over the past 7 days. Half is burned, half goes to bankers who stayed.

Tool 4 · Resolution fee curve

Fee = 2% + 58% × (pressure ÷ 10%)², capped at 60%. Pressure = W ÷ max(D + W, 10M), where W is tokens withdrawn over 7 days and D is what's still held at the bank. This flips the usual bank-run incentive: running first isn't free, and waiting pays.

08 · Housekeeping

Ghost bankers get shut down

An idle banker still collects a share of issuance, which dilutes everyone working. So the system removes them.

Report

Any wallet inactive for 30 days can be reported by anyone.

Bounty

The informant earns 2% of the dormant balance, capped at 100,000 tokens.

Revocation

The ghost pays a 70% fee, deliberately worse than the 60% maximum exit fee. Half burns, half pays active bankers.

Shutdown

Branches close, the charter burns, and the remaining 30% goes to the wallet.

Staying active is free: buying a license, depositing, withdrawing, reporting another ghost, or calling the zero-cost check-in all reset the clock. Transferring a charter does not, but the new holder gets a fresh 30-day grace period.

09 · Fees & vaults

Volatility becomes balance sheet

Buys and sells both pay ETH fees, and charter auctions add more. Each epoch's net flow decides where 70% of it goes.

The contraction vault is rate-limited: each hourly tick spends the smaller of 10% of its balance or 0.2% of pool reserves, which bounds buybacks near 5% of pool depth per day at launch settings. It can only buy, never sell.

10 · Who controls what

Immutable code, adjustable dials

The contracts can't be upgraded, but not every parameter is frozen. This is the part to read closely.

Locked forever
  • 1B hard cap
  • 700k/day base-rate ceiling (can only ratchet down)
  • Multiplier rule
  • Resolution fee curve
  • 70% revocation fee
  • 10% ceiling on manual taxes
  • 90% → 2%/3% launch tax decay
Owner-tunable in bounds
  • Epoch length (1–7 days)
  • Tax rates (≤10%)
  • Licenses per period (≤2,000)
  • Charters per day (≤100)
  • Team fee share (≤20%)
  • Auction floors and half-lives

The whitepaper states owner transactions carry no protocol-enforced delay, notice period, or cancellation window.

One-way switches
  • Charter transfers (off, then on forever)
  • Permissionless buyback execution (owner-cranked, then open forever)
  • Optional guardian can only pause auctions and vault purchases, never withdrawals or funds
